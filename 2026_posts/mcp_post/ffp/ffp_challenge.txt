How the Government Could Use FFP to Spur True Agentic AI Innovation

Odd right?  A firm fixed price contract could be the catalyst to spur agentic AI growth.  And the U.S. government is in a prime position to push agentic AI to the next level - not private Industry. It may even lead to a switch from the request for proposal model (tell us what you can do to solve a problem at your price) to firm fixed price (here is what we want, exactly, specifically at this set price).

It certainly could (and probably will!). Here is how it will unfold.  

The government established a pilot project.  It lists out initially a set of open source, unclassified FFP projects defined by government analysts and software engineers as follows:

- Provide an open source data set of interest,
- Provide a set of use cases and requirements for software that uses the data,
- Provide a cloud environment developers may work in,
- Provide a limited budget via OpenRouter for any model developers wish to use,
- Publish an API and interfaces,
- Publish a set of unit and functional tests,
- Provide a target deployment environment (the hardware, processor, etc. where the finished product lives)
- Provide target performance metrics,
- Provide security tests and harnesses, and 
- Provide a hidden evaluation set.

The last piece of the puzzle, provide the firm fixed price.  But the government makes the price very budget conscious.  Ridiculously budget conscious.  If an engineering team that assembled the above specification were to look at the requirements and say "We could not possibly do this for less than $250K even with agentic AI." - then set the price at $75k.  Set it low.  Very low.

Why?  The agentic AI tools exist to streamline software development.  The results are there.  What isn't there is the motivation to streamline them.  This is the motivation to truly streamlines and innovates their use. It's about motivating the best engineers to be creative.  To do more, to do better, with less in a constrained environment.

'Are you really that good?' is the question posed to them with this FFP approach.  Could a team a two or three engineers take agentic AI, create skills, apply them successfully and get a dependable and reliable solution up and running in five weeks where they each get $25K?  If these same engineers perform at peak efficiency for just 20 weeks of the year - they would net $200K.

Tell me this would not stimulate innovation of how to use and apply agentic AI tools.  The potential is there, but the model to incentivize what could be done really does not exist yet.
